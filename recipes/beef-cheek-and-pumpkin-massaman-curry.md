# Beef Cheek and Pumpkin Massaman Curry

A slow-braised massaman where beef cheeks cook down until they fall apart in a rich, spiced coconut sauce. Kabocha pumpkin goes in near the end so it holds its shape and soaks up the curry.

**Serves:** 4–6  
**Prep time:** 25 min  
**Cook time:** 3–3½ hours  

---

## Ingredients

### Beef
- 1.2 kg **beef cheeks** (about 4), trimmed of silverskin and cut into large chunks (5 cm)  
- 1 tsp **salt**  
- 2 tbsp **neutral oil**  

### Curry
- 2 x 400 ml cans **coconut milk** (don't shake — you'll use the thick cream on top separately)  
- 4–5 tbsp **massaman curry paste** (see [Massaman Curry Paste](massaman-curry-paste.md)) or store-bought  
- 500 ml **beef or chicken stock**  
- 1 **cinnamon stick**  
- 4 **green cardamom pods**, lightly crushed  
- 2 **bay leaves**  
- 3–4 **kaffir lime leaves**, torn  
- 8 **small shallots** (or 1 onion), peeled and halved  
- 2 tbsp **tamarind paste** (or concentrate, to taste)  
- 2–3 tbsp **fish sauce**  
- 2 tbsp **palm sugar** (or brown sugar)  

### Pumpkin & finish
- 600 g **kabocha or butternut pumpkin**, peeled and cut into 4 cm chunks  
- ½ cup **roasted unsalted peanuts**  
- Juice of ½ **lime**  
- **Fresh coriander** and sliced **red chili**, to garnish  
- Steamed **jasmine rice** or roti, to serve  

---

## Instructions

1. **Sear the beef:** Pat the beef cheeks dry and season with salt. Heat oil in a heavy Dutch oven over medium-high heat and brown the beef in batches, 2–3 minutes per side. Set aside.  
2. **Fry the paste:** Lower heat to medium. Spoon the thick coconut cream from the top of one can into the pot and simmer for 2–3 minutes until the oil begins to separate. Add the curry paste and fry, stirring, for 2–3 minutes until deeply fragrant.  
3. **Build the braise:** Add the remaining coconut milk, stock, cinnamon, cardamom, bay leaves, lime leaves, tamarind, 2 tbsp fish sauce, and palm sugar. Return the beef and any juices. Bring to a gentle simmer.  
4. **Braise:** Cover with the lid slightly ajar and cook on the lowest heat (or in a 150°C / 300°F oven) for 2½ hours, stirring occasionally, until the beef is nearly fork-tender.  
5. **Add shallots and pumpkin:** Stir in the shallots and pumpkin. Simmer uncovered for 25–30 minutes until the pumpkin is just tender and the sauce has thickened. Add a splash of stock if it gets too thick.  
6. **Finish:** Stir in the peanuts and lime juice. Taste and balance — more fish sauce for salt, palm sugar for sweetness, tamarind for sourness. Remove the cinnamon stick and bay leaves.  
7. **Serve:** Garnish with coriander and chili. Serve with jasmine rice or roti.  

---

### 💡 Notes
- **Beef cheeks** are worth the wait — their connective tissue melts into the sauce, giving a silky body that leaner cuts can't match. Brisket or chuck work as substitutes (reduce braising time by about 30 min).  
- **Kabocha** holds its shape better than butternut; if using butternut, add it 10 minutes later.  
- Like most braises, this tastes even better the next day. Skim any excess fat off the top once chilled.
