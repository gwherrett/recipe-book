# Potato-Leek Soup

**Source:** Serious Eats — J. Kenji López-Alt

**Prep Time:** 5 minutes
**Cook Time:** 40 minutes
**Yield:** 6 servings

An easy potato-leek soup that takes no shortcuts. Leeks are sweated slowly in butter, the potatoes are riced back into a blended broth for a silky but not gluey texture, and a lighter mix of milk, buttermilk and yogurt replaces the original's cream.

---

## Ingredients

- 2 tablespoons unsalted butter
- 2 large leeks, white and pale green parts only, rinsed and roughly chopped
- 1 quart homemade or store-bought low-sodium chicken stock
- 2 medium russet potatoes, peeled and cut into quarters (about ¾ pound)
- 1 bay leaf
- Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
- ¾ cup whole milk
- ½ cup buttermilk
- ¼ cup plain whole-milk Greek yogurt (or regular plain yogurt)
- ½ teaspoon freshly ground nutmeg
- Sliced chives or scallions, for serving

---

## Directions

1. **Sweat the leeks:** Heat butter in a large saucepan or Dutch oven over medium heat until melted. Add leeks, reduce heat to low, and cook, stirring frequently, until very soft but not browned, 10 to 15 minutes.

2. **Simmer the potatoes:** Add stock, potatoes, and bay leaf, and season lightly with salt and pepper. Bring to a boil over high heat, reduce to a gentle simmer, cover, and cook until potatoes are fall-apart tender, about 15 minutes.

### To Finish With a Ricer (Recommended)

3. **Blend the broth:** Remove potatoes from soup using tongs and transfer to a bowl. Set aside. Discard bay leaf. Transfer remaining soup to a blender. Slowly increase blender speed to high and blend until completely smooth, about 2 minutes. Return soup to a clean pot.

4. **Rice the potatoes:** Press potatoes through a potato ricer or food mill into the pot with the soup. Whisk in milk. Whisking frequently, bring soup to a simmer over medium-high heat, then remove from heat.

5. **Finish with buttermilk and yogurt:** In a bowl, whisk buttermilk and yogurt until smooth. Whisk in a ladleful of the hot soup, then whisk the mixture back into the pot. Do not let the soup boil again or it may curdle; rewarm gently over low heat if needed. Whisk in grated nutmeg. Season to taste with salt and pepper and serve with chives or scallions.

### To Finish With a Blender (Faster)

3. **Blend everything:** Add milk to pot. Discard bay leaf. Working in batches if necessary, transfer soup to a blender. Slowly increase blender speed to high and blend until completely smooth, about 2 minutes. Return soup to a clean pot, pressing it through a fine-mesh strainer with the bottom of a ladle if a smoother texture is desired.

4. **Finish:** Whisking frequently, bring soup to a simmer over medium-high heat, then remove from heat. In a bowl, whisk buttermilk and yogurt until smooth, whisk in a ladleful of the hot soup, then whisk the mixture back into the pot. Do not let the soup boil again. Alternatively, chill the soup completely, then whisk in the buttermilk and yogurt and serve cold. Whisk in grated nutmeg. Season to taste with salt and pepper and serve with chives or scallions.

---

## Special Equipment

- Ricer, blender (optional), fine-mesh strainer (optional)

---

## Notes

- For best results, follow instructions using a ricer. Blender soup will still taste good, but will have a slightly gluier consistency.
- **Lighter version:** Adapted from the original, which uses 1 cup buttermilk and ½ cup heavy cream. Milk replaces the cream's volume, and yogurt brings back some of its body. Greek yogurt gives the creamiest result. Because there's no cream to stabilize the dairy, the buttermilk and yogurt go in off the heat.
