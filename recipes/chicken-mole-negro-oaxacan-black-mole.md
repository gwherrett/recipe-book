# Chicken Mole Negro (Oaxacan Black Mole)

**Source:** [Rick Bayless, via Sonoma Magazine (May 2010)](https://www.sonomamag.com/robledos-in-a-state/)

**Total Time:** about 4 hours (sauce can be made days ahead)
**Yield:** 8 servings, with about 10 cups of sauce (leftovers for enchiladas)

Rick Bayless' Oaxacan black mole, served at the 2010 White House state dinner honouring Mexican President Calderón. Four separate purées — nuts, green tomatoes, banana-spice, and chiles — are each fried down until nearly black, then simmered with broth and Mexican chocolate. This version is adapted to use anchos, guajillos, canned chipotle, pine nuts, and home-roasted jack-o'-lantern pumpkin seeds (see Substitutions below).

---

## Ingredients

### Chiles

- 11 medium (about 5½ oz) dried ancho chiles
- About 2 oz (6 to 8 medium) dried guajillo chiles
- 6 medium (about 2 oz) dried pasilla chiles (see note on checking these)
- 1 canned chipotle chile in adobo
- 1 corn tortilla, torn into small pieces

### Seeds & Nuts

- ½ cup sesame seeds, plus a few extra for garnish
- ⅓ cup roasted, seasoned pumpkin seeds (pepitas) in their shells
- ¼ cup unskinned or Spanish peanuts
- ¼ cup pine nuts

### Purée Base

- 2 ¼-inch-thick slices white onion
- 4 garlic cloves, unpeeled
- 1 lb (2 medium-large or 6 to 8 plum) green tomatoes, roughly chopped
- 4 oz (2 to 3 medium) tomatillos, husked, rinsed and roughly chopped
- 2 slices stale bread, toasted until very dark
- ¼ tsp cloves, preferably freshly ground
- ½ tsp black pepper, preferably freshly ground
- ½ tsp cinnamon, preferably freshly ground Mexican canela
- A scant 1 tsp oregano, preferably Mexican
- ½ tsp dried thyme
- ½ ripe banana

### To Finish

- About 2 cups rich-tasting lard or vegetable oil (for frying the chiles)
- About 10 cups chicken broth
- ½ cup (about 3 oz) finely chopped Mexican chocolate
- 2 or 3 avocado leaves (if you have them)
- Salt, about 1 tbsp depending on the saltiness of the broth (start lower — see Notes)
- Sugar, about ¼ cup (or a little more)
- 2 large (3½ to 4 lb) chickens, cut into quarters

---

## Instructions

### 1. Getting Started

1. **Seed the chiles:** Pull the stems (and attached seed pods) from the dried anchos, guajillos and pasillas, tear them open and shake or scrape out the seeds, collecting them as you go. Set the canned chipotle aside.

2. **Burn the seeds:** Scoop the seeds into an ungreased 8- to 9-inch skillet with the torn tortilla. Set over medium heat, turn on the exhaust fan, open a window, and toast, shaking the pan regularly, until thoroughly burned to charcoal black, about 15 minutes. (This is very important to the flavour and colour of the mole.) Scrape into a fine-mesh strainer, rinse for about 30 seconds, then transfer to a blender.

3. **Roast the onion and garlic:** Set an ungreased skillet or griddle over medium heat, lay on a piece of foil, and lay the onion slices and garlic on it. Roast until soft and very dark — about 5 minutes per side for the onion (peel it off the foil to turn it) and about 15 minutes for the garlic, turning frequently. Cool the garlic a bit, peel, and combine with the onion in a large bowl.

4. **Fry and soak the chiles:** Meanwhile, heat the oven to 350°F. Return the skillet to medium heat, add a scant 2 cups of the lard or oil (about ½-inch depth), and when hot, fry the dried chiles a couple at a time. They'll unfurl quickly and release their aroma; after about 30 seconds they'll have lightened in colour and be well toasted (crisp when cool, but not burnt-smelling). Drain well, gather into a large bowl, cover with hot tap water and let rehydrate 30 minutes, stirring regularly. Drain, reserving the soaking liquid.

5. **Toast the seeds and nuts:** While the chiles soak, spread the sesame seeds on one baking sheet and the peanuts on another; put both in the oven. In about 12 minutes the sesame will be dark brown; the peanuts take slightly longer. Pine nuts toast much faster — add them to the peanut sheet for the last 5 to 7 minutes and watch closely. The pumpkin seeds are already roasted; just warm them on the tray for the last few minutes.

6. **Nut purée:** Add the sesame (reserving a few for garnish), pumpkin seeds, peanuts and pine nuts to the blender with the burnt seeds and tortilla, plus 1½ cups of the broth. Blend as smooth as you can — give it extra time to break down the pumpkin seed shells. Transfer to a small bowl.

7. **Tomato purée:** Without rinsing the blender, purée the green tomatoes and tomatillos with another ½ cup of the broth. Pour into another bowl.

8. **Banana-spice purée:** Again without rinsing, blend the roasted onion and garlic with the toasted bread, cloves, black pepper, cinnamon, oregano, thyme, banana and ¾ cup broth until smooth. Pour into a small bowl.

9. **Chile purée:** Without rinsing, add half the soaked chiles, the canned chipotle, and ½ cup of the soaking liquid; blend smooth and pour into another bowl. Repeat with the remaining chiles and another ½ cup soaking liquid.

### 2. From Four Purées to Mole

1. **Fry the tomato purée:** In a very large (8- to 9-quart) pot, preferably a Dutch oven or cazuela, heat 3 tbsp of the lard or oil (some of the chile-frying fat is fine) over medium-high. When very hot, add the tomato purée and stir and scrape with a flat wooden spatula for 15 to 20 minutes, until reduced, thick as tomato paste, and the colour of cinnamon stick.

2. **Add the nut purée:** Stir and scrape until reduced, thick and dark again — the colour of black olive paste — about 8 minutes.

3. **Add the banana-spice purée:** Stir and scrape another 7 to 8 minutes, until it simmers back down to a thick mass about the same colour as before.

4. **Add the chile purée:** Stir well and reduce over medium-low until very thick and almost black, about 30 minutes, stirring regularly (but not constantly).

5. **Simmer:** Stir in the remaining 7 cups of broth, the chocolate and the avocado leaves (if using). Partially cover and simmer gently about 1 hour. Season with salt and sugar — this is a sweet mole, and the sugar balances the dark, toasty flavours. Remove the avocado leaves.

6. **Blend and strain:** In batches, in a loosely covered blender, purée until as smooth as possible, then press through a medium-mesh strainer into a large bowl. This is where any remaining bits of pumpkin seed shell get caught.

### 3. Finishing the Dish

1. **Braise the chicken:** Return the mole to the pot and bring to a simmer. Nestle in the leg-and-thigh quarters, partially cover and cook 15 minutes, then add the breast quarters, partially cover and simmer 20 to 25 minutes more, until all the chicken is done.

2. **Serve:** Lift the chicken onto a large warm platter with a slotted spoon. Spoon a generous amount of mole over and around, sprinkle with the reserved sesame seeds, and serve.

---

## Advance Preparation

The mole can be completed through Step 2 several days ahead — it gets better, in fact. Cover and refrigerate. Complete Step 3 shortly before serving.

## Substitutions from the Original

| Original (Bayless) | This version | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 11 dried mulato chiles (5½ oz) | 11 dried ancho chiles (5½ oz) | Mulatos are hard to find; anchos are the closest, slightly fruitier and a touch lighter in colour |
| 6 dried chilhuacle chiles (2 oz) | About 2 oz dried guajillo chiles | Chilhuacles are rarely available outside Oaxaca |
| 1 dried chipotle meco | 1 canned chipotle in adobo | Added straight to the chile purée — not fried or soaked |
| ¼ cup pecan halves | ⅓ cup roasted, seasoned jack-o'-lantern pumpkin seeds (in shell) | Brings in pepitas; extra volume allows for the shells |
| ¼ cup unskinned almonds | ¼ cup pine nuts | Uses what's on hand; pine nuts are common in Mexican moles |

Bayless' own note for missing chilhuacles is to use 6 oz (12) mulatos, 2½ oz (8) pasillas and 1 oz (4) guajillos instead.

## Notes

- **Check your pasillas:** Some stores label anchos as "pasilla." True pasillas (chile negro) are long, skinny, wrinkled and nearly black. If yours are wide and heart-shaped, they're anchos — just use them as extra base chile; the mole will be a little less bitter and earthy.
- **Seasoned pumpkin seeds:** Because the seeds are already salted and spiced, start with about half the salt at the end and adjust up. Garlic, chili or smoky seasonings all suit the mole.
- **Pumpkin seed shells:** Blend the nut purée longer than usual, and don't skip the final straining — the medium-mesh strainer removes the shell fibre.
