# Beef & Chouriço Lasagna

A variation of [Meat Lasagna (All-Day)](meat-lasagna-all-day.md) made with ground beef and Portuguese chouriço. The sofrito is cooked first and set aside so the meat can be cooked on its own, its fat managed, and its cooking slowed with milk and wine to keep it tender before everything comes together.

## Ingredients

### Ragù Bolognese
- 2 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil
- 4 tablespoons butter
- 1 1/2 pounds ground beef (85/15)
- 1/2 pound semi-cured Portuguese chouriço (or Spanish chorizo), casing removed, cut into 1/4-inch dice
- 1 large onion, finely chopped (about 1 1/2 cups)
- 2 large carrots, peeled and cut into 1/4-inch dice (about 1 cup)
- 3 large ribs celery, peeled and cut into 1/4-inch dice (about 1 cup)
- 4 cloves garlic, finely minced
- 1/2 cup fresh sage leaves, finely chopped
- Large pinch red pepper flakes (optional)
- 1 (28-ounce) can crushed Italian plum tomatoes (preferably D.O.P. San Marzano)
- 1 1/2 cups dry red wine
- 1 1/2 cups whole milk
- 2 cups chicken or veal stock (or low-sodium canned chicken broth)
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 tablespoon fish sauce
- 1/2 cup heavy cream
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 1/2 cup minced basil or parsley (or a mix)

### Ricotta Mixture
- 3 cups whole-milk ricotta
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 2 large eggs
- 1/4 cup minced basil or parsley (or a mix)

### Besciamella
- 2 tablespoons butter
- 2 tablespoons flour
- 2 cloves garlic, finely minced
- 2 cups whole milk
- 1/2 pound low-moisture whole-milk mozzarella, grated
- 1/4 teaspoon freshly grated nutmeg
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper

### Assembly
- 15 sheets fresh pasta (4×8 inches) or 15 no-boil lasagna noodles
- 4 ounces Parmigiano-Reggiano, grated (about 2 cups)
- 2 tablespoons minced basil or parsley (or a mix)

## Instructions

### Ragù Bolognese
1. **Sofrito:** Heat butter and olive oil in a large Dutch oven over medium heat until butter stops foaming. Add onions, carrots, and celery; cook, stirring frequently, until softened but not browned, 8–10 minutes. Add garlic for the last minute. Stop when the vegetables are nearly done — they will finish cooking later. Scoop the sofrito into a bowl, leaving as much fat in the pot as you can.
2. **Meat:** Return the pot to medium-low heat. Add the diced chouriço and cook, stirring, until it starts to release its red fat, 2–3 minutes (don't let it crisp). Raise heat to medium, add the ground beef, and cook gently, breaking up the meat, just until it loses its raw pink colour, 5–7 minutes. Do not brown hard — high heat tightens the beef and makes it gristly. Tilt the pot and spoon off excess fat, leaving 1–2 tablespoons (chouriço fat adds flavour).
3. **Milk, then wine:** Add milk and bring to a gentle simmer, stirring, until mostly absorbed, about 10–15 minutes. Add wine and simmer until most of the alcohol smell has cooked off and the liquid has reduced by about half, about 10 minutes. The liquid slows the cooking and keeps the meat tender.
4. **Sofrito back in:** Return the sofrito to the pot along with the sage, red pepper flakes, and bay leaves. Stir and cook together for 3–5 minutes so the flavours meld.
5. **Tomatoes:** Add tomatoes and stock; bring to a simmer. Reduce heat to low, partially cover, and simmer 2 1/2–3 hours, stirring occasionally, until liquid is just below the level of the meat (about 2 quarts of sauce). Add extra stock if needed.
6. **Finish:** Remove bay leaves. Add fish sauce and heavy cream; simmer until fat is emulsified, about 5 minutes. Season with salt and pepper. Remove from heat and rest 30 minutes. Stir in herbs. (Bolognese keeps up to 1 week in the fridge and improves with time; reheat before using.)

### Ricotta Mixture
7. Process or whisk ricotta until smooth. Season with salt and pepper. Add eggs and herbs; mix until incorporated. Set aside.

### Besciamella
8. Melt butter in a medium saucepan over medium-high heat until foaming subsides, about 1 minute. Add flour and whisk until light blond and slightly nutty, about 1 minute. Add garlic and stir to combine.
9. Whisking constantly, pour in milk in a steady stream until fully incorporated. Bring to a simmer until thickened. Remove from heat, add mozzarella and nutmeg, and whisk until melted. Return to a simmer briefly, then remove from heat and season with salt and pepper.

### Assembly
10. If using no-boil noodles, soak in a baking dish with hot water for 10 minutes, changing water once. Drain and pat dry on kitchen towels.
11. Preheat oven to 375°F (190°C) with racks on the lower-middle and lowest positions.
12. Spread 1/6 of the ragù (about 1 1/3 cups) on the bottom of a 13×9-inch baking dish; drizzle with 1/2 cup besciamella. Layer 3 pasta sheets on top.
13. **Layer 2:** 1/6 ragù, 1/2 cup besciamella, 1/3 cup Parmesan, pasta.
14. **Layer 3:** 1/2 of ricotta mixture, 1/6 ragù, 1/3 cup Parmesan, pasta.
15. **Layer 4:** 1/6 ragù, 1/2 cup besciamella, 1/3 cup Parmesan, pasta.
16. **Layer 5:** remaining ricotta mixture, 1/6 ragù, 1/3 cup Parmesan, pasta.
17. **Top:** remaining ragù, remaining besciamella (about 1 cup), remaining 2/3 cup Parmesan.
18. Place a foil-lined rimmed baking sheet on the lower rack to catch drips. Bake lasagna on the upper rack until edges are crisping and top is bubbly and golden brown, about 45 minutes, rotating halfway. Rest 10 minutes, sprinkle with herbs, and serve.

## Notes
- For the best ricotta, make fresh or use a brand with no gums or stabilizers (milk, salt, and acid/starter only).
- A layer of fat may form on top of the ragù during its long simmer — do not skim it off; it emulsifies in at the end.
- The ragù can be made up to a week ahead and refrigerated; it improves with time.
- Semi-cured chouriço goes in first so its paprika-rich fat seasons the beef; dicing it small lets it soften into the ragù over the long simmer. If using fresh (raw) Mexican-style chorizo instead, remove the casings and add it with the beef.
- Cooking the sofrito first and setting it aside lets you cook the meat on its own and control how much fat stays in the sauce.
- Adding milk and wine as soon as the meat loses its pink keeps the beef from overcooking and turning gristly. Milk first is the classic Bolognese order; it also mellows the chouriço.
- Chouriço is usually milder than Spanish chorizo and often smoked, so taste after the sofrito goes back in and add red pepper flakes if you want more heat.
