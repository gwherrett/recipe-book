# Cantonese Smoked Rotisserie Goose

A blend of three methods, adapted for a rotisserie on a gas barbecue:

- **Hong Kong–style roast goose:** five-spice and soy cavity marinade, honey-vinegar skin, maltose glaze.
- **Geoff's smoked goose:** poultry-mix rub, apple or cherry smoke.
- **Serious Eats crisp-skinned goose:** a full boiling-water blanch, a salt and baking powder dry brine, and a long, low render.

A 36–48 hour dry brine in the fridge dries the skin of a store-bought, farm-raised bird so it renders and crisps on the spit.

## Timeline

| When | Step |
|------|------|
| 2 days before | Trim, prick, blanch, cavity marinade, dry brine — fridge uncovered |
| Night before | Brush skin with honey-vinegar — back in fridge uncovered |
| Cook day | Truss, spit, smoke ~1.5 h, low render ~1–1.5 h, glaze and crisp 30–40 min, rest 20–30 min |

## Cooking Instructions

1. **Trim the Goose (2 days before)**:
   - Remove the giblets and neck. Cut off the wing tips at the joint — on a spit they burn before the bird is done. Remove the wishbone for easier carving (same method as a turkey). Save the neck, wing tips, wishbone and giblets for the jus.
   - Trim the loose neck skin and pull out the pale, lumpy fat pads inside the cavity. Save them for rendering.

2. **Prick the Skin**:
   - With a trussing needle or the tip of a paring knife, prick the skin at **1 cm (½ in) intervals** all over, front and back, without piercing the meat. Concentrate on the fattiest areas — under the wings and around the thighs. This lets the fat escape on the spit.

3. **Blanch**:
   - Bring a large stockpot two-thirds full of water to a boil. Wearing clean rubber gloves, hold the goose by the legs and lower it neck end first until half submerged, for **1 minute**. Lift it out, drain, then hold it by the wings and submerge the tail end for **1 minute**.
   - The skin will tighten and start to render. Pat completely dry, inside and out.
   - *If the bird won't fit in your pot:* set it on a rack in the sink and slowly pour about **3 L boiling water** over the skin, turning to cover all sides.

4. **Season the Cavity**:
   - Mix **2 tbsp salt, 1 tbsp five-spice powder, 2 tbsp Shaoxing wine, 1 tbsp light soy and 1 tbsp dark soy**. Rub all over the inside of the cavity.
   - Stuff the cavity with **2 star anise, 4 slices ginger and 3 scallions**. Keep soy off the skin — it darkens and burns unevenly.

5. **Dry Brine the Skin**:
   - Combine **2 tbsp Diamond Crystal kosher salt** (or 4 tsp Morton's), **2 tsp baking powder, 1 tsp five-spice powder and 1 tbsp Geoff's Poultry Mix**.
   - Sprinkle evenly over all the skin, including under the wings and legs. Use just enough to coat — you may not need it all.
   - Set the goose breast side up on a wire rack over a tray and refrigerate **uncovered for 36–48 hours**.

6. **Honey-Vinegar Skin (night before)**:
   - Wipe off any visible white residue from the dry brine.
   - Whisk **2 tbsp honey and 2 tbsp red rice vinegar** into **1 cup boiling water**. Brush all over the skin in thin, even coats.
   - Return to the fridge uncovered overnight until the skin is taut, dry and slightly translucent.

7. **Truss and Spit (cook day)**:
   - Sew the cavity shut with butcher's twine or close it with trussing skewers — the spit turns the bird upside down, and the marinade will otherwise drain onto the burners.
   - Tie the wings and legs tight to the body. Run the spit through the centre, secure the forks, and **check the balance** by rolling the spit on your palms. Adjust until it doesn't flop to one side.
   - Let the goose stand at room temperature 30–45 minutes while the barbecue heats.

8. **Set Up the Gas Barbecue**:
   - Remove the cooking grates. Place a drip pan with about **2.5 cm (1 in) warm water** directly under where the goose will turn.
   - Fill a smoker box with **apple or cherry wood chips** (soaked 30 minutes) and set it over the far-side burner. Light that burner on high until the chips smoke, then turn it to medium.
   - Leave the burners under the goose **off** (indirect heat). Use the rear rotisserie burner on medium if you have one; otherwise use the opposite end burner on low to medium.
   - Close the lid and bring the barbecue to **250–275°F**.

9. **Smoke (first 60–90 minutes)**:
   - Mount the spit, start the motor and close the lid. Hold **250–275°F**, topping up the chips every 30–40 minutes.
   - Smoke takes best on the bare, dry skin, so do all smoking in this phase.

10. **Low Render (next 60–90 minutes)**:
    - Let the smoker box burn out and hold the barbecue at **275–300°F**. Low, steady heat renders out the fat under the skin without drying the meat.
    - At the changeover, carefully pull the drip pan. Goose renders 2–3 cups of fat. Pour the fat and water into a large bowl to separate, then return the pan with fresh warm water to prevent flare-ups.
    - Insert a probe thermometer into the thickest part of the thigh, away from the bone and spit.

11. **Glaze and Crisp (last 30–40 minutes)**:
    - When the thigh reads about **165°F**, loosen **1 tbsp maltose** (or honey) with **1 tbsp hot water**. Brush onto the turning goose 2–3 times, 10 minutes apart.
    - Turn the rear burner (or end burner) to high and raise the barbecue to about **375–425°F** until the skin is deep mahogany and crisp. Watch closely — sugar burns fast.
    - The goose is done when the thigh reads **at least 175°F** and the breast **165°F**. Total cook time is roughly 3–3.5 hours for a 4–5 kg bird.

12. **Rest, Chop and Serve**:
    - Lift the spit off, remove the forks and twine, and rest the goose for **20–30 minutes**. Remove the aromatics from the cavity.
    - Chop through the bone into bite-sized pieces with a heavy cleaver, Cantonese style. Serve with the Shaoxing goose jus or plum sauce.

## Shaoxing Goose Jus (optional)

Make this while the goose dry-brines; it keeps 5 days in the fridge.

1. Cut the neck and wing tips into 7 cm (3 in) pieces. In a saucepan, render **1 tbsp minced goose fat** over medium heat. Add the neck, wing tips, wishbone, heart and gizzard (keep the liver aside), and brown well, 8–10 minutes.
2. Add **1 sliced onion, 4 slices ginger and 2 scallions**. Cook until the onion browns, about 10 minutes.
3. Deglaze with **½ cup Shaoxing wine**. Add **2 cups low-sodium chicken stock, 1 cup water, 1 star anise and 1 tbsp light soy**. Simmer, partly covered, for 2 hours, topping up with water to keep the bones mostly submerged.
4. Strain, pressing on the solids, and skim off the fat. You should have about 2 cups of stock.
5. To finish, cook **2 tbsp rendered goose fat** with **2 tbsp flour** until light brown, about 5 minutes. Gradually whisk in the hot stock. Simmer until slightly thickened, 3–6 minutes.
6. Add the finely chopped liver if you like and simmer 1 minute more. Season with salt and white pepper.

## Ingredients

**Goose**
- **1 whole goose** (4–5 kg), thawed; wing tips, wishbone, neck and giblets removed and reserved

**Cavity marinade**
- **2 tbsp salt**
- **1 tbsp five-spice powder**
- **2 tbsp Shaoxing wine**
- **1 tbsp light soy sauce**
- **1 tbsp dark soy sauce**
- **2 star anise**
- **4 slices ginger**
- **3 scallions**

**Dry brine (skin)**
- **2 tbsp Diamond Crystal kosher salt** (or 4 tsp Morton's)
- **2 tsp baking powder**
- **1 tsp five-spice powder**
- **1 tbsp Geoff's Poultry Mix** (sage, thyme, rosemary)

**Honey-vinegar skin**
- **2 tbsp honey**
- **2 tbsp red rice vinegar**
- **1 cup boiling water**

**Maltose glaze**
- **1 tbsp maltose** (or honey)
- **1 tbsp hot water**

**Shaoxing goose jus (optional)**
- Reserved neck, wing tips, wishbone and giblets
- **1 tbsp minced goose fat**, plus **2 tbsp rendered goose fat**
- **1 onion**, sliced
- **4 slices ginger**
- **2 scallions**
- **½ cup Shaoxing wine**
- **2 cups low-sodium chicken stock**
- **1 cup water**
- **1 star anise**
- **1 tbsp light soy sauce**
- **2 tbsp all-purpose flour**
- **Salt and white pepper**, to taste

**Equipment**
- Rotisserie spit and motor
- Large stockpot and clean rubber gloves, for blanching
- Smoker box and **apple or cherry wood chips**, soaked 30 minutes
- Drip pan
- Butcher's twine or trussing skewers
- Wire rack and tray, for the fridge
- Probe thermometer
- Heavy cleaver

## Notes

- **Why the soy stays inside:** soy and sugar on the skin burn and colour unevenly over a long cook. The skin gets only dry salt, baking powder and spice until the honey-vinegar brush.
- **Why baking powder:** it raises the skin's pH, which helps it brown and blister crisp (Serious Eats technique).
- **Why blanch and render low:** blanching tightens the skin and starts the render. The long stretch at 275–300°F melts out the thick fat layer before the high-heat finish crisps the skin.
- **Why glaze only at the end:** basting with maltose every 30 minutes, as an oven recipe does, will char on a gas grill over 3 hours.
- **Rendered fat:** strain the drip-pan fat and the trimmed cavity fat (rendered slowly in a pan) and keep it for roast potatoes.
