# Cantonese Smoked Rotisserie Goose

A blend of Hong Kong–style roast goose (five-spice and soy cavity marinade, honey-vinegar skin, maltose glaze) with Geoff's smoked goose (poultry-mix dry rub, apple or cherry smoke), adapted for a rotisserie on a gas barbecue. A 36–48 hour dry brine in the fridge dries the skin of a store-bought, farm-raised bird so it renders and crisps on the spit.

## Timeline

| When | Step |
|------|------|
| 2 days before | Prick, scald, cavity marinade, dry brine — fridge uncovered |
| Night before | Brush skin with honey-vinegar — back in fridge uncovered |
| Cook day | Truss, spit, smoke ~1–1.5 h, roast ~1–1.5 h, glaze and crisp 30–40 min, rest 20 min |

## Cooking Instructions

1. **Prepare the Goose (2 days before)**:
   - Remove the giblets and neck; trim the loose neck skin and the large fat pads inside the cavity (save them for rendering).
   - Pat dry. With a sharp skewer or the tip of a knife, prick the skin all over — especially over the breast and thighs — without piercing the meat. This lets the fat escape on the spit.

2. **Scald the Skin**:
   - Set the goose on a rack in the sink and slowly pour about **2 L boiling water** over the skin, turning to cover all sides. The skin will tighten and shrink. Pat completely dry.

3. **Season the Cavity**:
   - Mix **2 tbsp salt, 1 tbsp five-spice powder, 2 tbsp Shaoxing wine, 1 tbsp light soy and 1 tbsp dark soy**. Rub all over the inside of the cavity.
   - Stuff the cavity with **2 star anise, 4 slices ginger and 3 scallions**. Keep soy off the skin — it darkens and burns unevenly.

4. **Dry Brine the Skin**:
   - Combine **2 tbsp kosher salt, 1 tsp five-spice powder and 1 tbsp Geoff's Poultry Mix**. Rub evenly over all the skin, including under the wings and legs.
   - Set the goose breast side up on a wire rack over a tray and refrigerate **uncovered for 36–48 hours**.

5. **Honey-Vinegar Skin (night before)**:
   - Whisk **2 tbsp honey and 2 tbsp red rice vinegar** into **1 cup boiling water**. Brush all over the skin in thin, even coats.
   - Return to the fridge uncovered overnight until the skin is taut, dry and slightly translucent.

6. **Truss and Spit (cook day)**:
   - Sew the cavity shut with butcher's twine or close it with trussing skewers — the spit turns the bird upside down, and the marinade will otherwise drain onto the burners.
   - Tie the wings and legs tight to the body. Run the spit through the centre, secure the forks, and **check the balance** by rolling the spit on your palms; adjust until it doesn't flop to one side.
   - Let the goose stand at room temperature 30–45 minutes while the barbecue heats.

7. **Set Up the Gas Barbecue**:
   - Remove the cooking grates. Place a drip pan with about **2.5 cm (1 in) water** directly under where the goose will turn.
   - Fill a smoker box with **apple or cherry wood chips** (soaked 30 minutes) and set it over the far-side burner. Light that burner on high until the chips smoke, then turn it to medium.
   - Leave the burners under the goose **off** (indirect heat). Use the rear rotisserie burner on medium if you have one; otherwise use the opposite end burner on low to medium.
   - Close the lid and bring the barbecue to **250–275°F**.

8. **Smoke (first 60–90 minutes)**:
   - Mount the spit, start the motor and close the lid. Hold **250–275°F**, topping up the chips every 30–40 minutes.
   - Smoke takes best on the bare, dry skin, so do all smoking in this phase.

9. **Roast (next 60–90 minutes)**:
   - Let the smoker box burn out. Raise the barbecue to **325–350°F**.
   - Check the drip pan at this changeover — goose renders 1–2 cups of fat. Ladle off fat and save it, and add water if the pan is running dry, to avoid flare-ups.
   - Insert a probe thermometer into the thickest part of the thigh, away from the bone and spit.

10. **Glaze and Crisp (last 30–40 minutes)**:
    - When the thigh reads about **160°F**, loosen **1 tbsp maltose** (or honey) with **1 tbsp hot water**. Brush onto the turning goose 2–3 times, 10 minutes apart.
    - Turn the rear burner (or end burner) to high and raise the barbecue to about **375°F** until the skin is deep mahogany and crisp. Watch closely — sugar burns fast.
    - The goose is done when the breast reaches **165°F** and the thigh **175°F**. Total cook time is roughly 2.5–3 hours for a 4–5 kg bird.

11. **Rest, Chop and Serve**:
    - Lift the spit off, remove the forks and twine, and rest the goose for **20 minutes**. Remove the aromatics from the cavity.
    - Chop through the bone into bite-sized pieces with a heavy cleaver, Cantonese style. Serve with plum sauce.

## Ingredients

**Goose**
- **1 whole goose** (4–5 kg), thawed, giblets removed

**Skin scald**
- **About 2 L boiling water**

**Cavity marinade**
- **2 tbsp salt**
- **1 tbsp five-spice powder**
- **2 tbsp Shaoxing wine**
- **1 tbsp light soy sauce**
- **1 tbsp dark soy sauce**
- **2 star anise**
- **4 slices ginger**
- **3 scallions**

**Dry brine (skin)**
- **2 tbsp kosher salt**
- **1 tsp five-spice powder**
- **1 tbsp Geoff's Poultry Mix** (sage, thyme, rosemary)

**Honey-vinegar skin**
- **2 tbsp honey**
- **2 tbsp red rice vinegar**
- **1 cup boiling water**

**Maltose glaze**
- **1 tbsp maltose** (or honey)
- **1 tbsp hot water**

**Equipment**
- Rotisserie spit and motor
- Smoker box and **apple or cherry wood chips**, soaked 30 minutes
- Drip pan
- Butcher's twine or trussing skewers
- Wire rack and tray, for the fridge
- Probe thermometer
- Heavy cleaver

## Notes

- **Why the soy stays inside:** soy and sugar on the skin burn and colour unevenly over a long cook. The skin gets only dry salt and spice until the honey-vinegar brush.
- **Why glaze only at the end:** basting with maltose every 30 minutes, as an oven recipe does, will char on a gas grill over 2–3 hours.
- **Rendered fat:** strain the drip-pan fat and the trimmed cavity fat (rendered slowly in a pan) and keep it for roast potatoes.
