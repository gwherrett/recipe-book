# Rotisserie Herb-Brined Cornish Hens (Scaled for 2 Birds)

## Ingredients

### Cornish Hens
- 2 Cornish hens (1–2 lbs each; ~1.5 lbs typical)

### Brine
- 1/4 cup table salt  
- 2 tbsp sugar  
- 1 quart water  

*(If you need more to submerge the hens, make additional brine using the same ratio:  
1 quart water : 1/4 cup salt : 2 tbsp sugar.)*

### Herb Rub
- 2 tsp fresh thyme, minced  
- 1 tsp fresh oregano, minced  
- 1 tsp fresh rosemary, minced  
- 1 tsp fresh sage, minced  
- 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper  
- Zest of 1/2 lemon (~3/4 tsp)  
- 1 clove garlic, minced  
- 1 tbsp extra virgin olive oil  

### Optional
- Lemon halves and herb sprigs, for the cavities  
- Applewood or cherry wood chunk, for light smoke  
- Honey–lemon glaze, for the last 10 minutes  

## Method

### 1. Brine the hens
1. Mix salt and sugar into the water until dissolved.  
2. Submerge hens fully in the brine.  
3. Refrigerate **1–4 hours** (no longer than 6, to avoid over-salting).

### 2. Prepare the herb rub & hens
1. Combine all herb rub ingredients in a small bowl.  
2. Remove hens from the brine and **pat completely dry**, inside and out.  
3. Loosen the breast skin gently with your fingers.  
4. Rub a small amount of herb mixture directly onto the breast meat under the skin.  
5. Rub the rest over the skin and inside the cavities. Stuff cavities with lemon halves and herb sprigs if you like.  
6. Truss both hens, tying legs and wings snugly so they spin evenly.  
7. Mount both hens on a **single rotisserie spit**:
   - First bird: **feet-first**  
   - Second bird: **breast-first**, so the backbones face each other  
   - Secure both with spit forks

### 3. Set up the grill
1. Set up your **Weber grill for indirect medium heat (350–375°F / 175–190°C)**.  
2. Place a **drip pan** under the rotisserie to catch juices.  
3. If using charcoal, bank coals on each side of the pan. If using gas, light the outer burners only.  
4. For subtle smoke, add a chunk of applewood or cherry wood to the coals.

### 4. Cook the hens
1. Start the rotisserie motor and close the lid.  
2. Cook for **30–45 minutes**, depending on size.  
   - For 1.5 lb hens, expect ~35 minutes; larger hens can take up to an hour.  
3. Begin checking internal temperature at 20 minutes, then every 10 minutes:  
   - **Breast:** 160–165°F  
   - **Thigh:** 170–180°F  
4. For extra colour, brush with a honey–lemon glaze during the last 10 minutes.  
5. Remove from the grill using heat-proof gloves.  
6. Transfer hens to a platter, tent with foil, and rest **15 minutes**.

### 5. Optional: Make a sauce from the drippings
1. If drippings are not burnt, skim off fat and pour into a small saucepan.  
2. Add **1 cup chicken stock** and bring to a simmer.  
3. Add any juices from carving.  
4. Reduce slightly and season to taste.  

### 6. Final prep & serve
1. Halve each hen:  
   - Cut along the backbone  
   - Split through the breast  
2. Serve **½ hen per person**, or a whole hen for hearty appetites, with grilled lemon wedges.

## Notes
- If using frozen hens, thaw fully before brining.
- Rosemary can overpower—use lightly.
- A sheet pan next to your platter helps catch drips during removal.
- Rotisserie potatoes in the drip pan are highly recommended.
