# Proper Italian Bolognese Sauce

A rich and traditional Italian meat sauce from Bologna, known as *Ragù alla Bolognese*. Slow-simmered with beef, pork, and pancetta for deep flavor, balanced with milk for richness. A larger porcini variation follows the main recipe.

## Ingredients

- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 medium onion, finely chopped
- 1 celery stalk, finely chopped
- 1 carrot, finely chopped
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 8 oz ground beef
- 8 oz ground pork
- 4 oz pancetta, finely chopped
- 1/2 cup dry white wine
- 1 cup whole milk
- 1 can (14 oz) crushed tomatoes
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 bay leaf
- 1/4 tsp ground nutmeg
- Salt and pepper to taste
- Freshly grated Parmesan cheese, for serving
- Cooked pasta of choice (tagliatelle or pappardelle recommended)

## Method

1. Heat olive oil in a heavy-bottomed pot over medium heat.
2. Add pancetta and cook until lightly browned and fat has rendered.
3. Add onion, celery, carrot, and garlic; sauté until softened (5–7 minutes).
4. Increase heat to medium-high. Add beef and pork, breaking apart with a wooden spoon until browned.
5. Pour in wine and simmer until mostly evaporated.
6. Stir in tomatoes, tomato paste, milk, bay leaf, and nutmeg. Season with salt and pepper.
7. Reduce heat to low, cover partially, and simmer gently for 2–3 hours, stirring occasionally.
8. Remove bay leaf, adjust seasoning, and serve over pasta with Parmesan.

## Variation: Porcini Bolognese with Tagliatelle (Large Batch)

A bigger, earthier batch: porcini and the vegetables are puréed into the base, red wine replaces white, and it's served with ricotta and basil. Serves about 8.

### Ingredients

- 2 oz dried porcini mushrooms, wiped of grit
- 1/4 lb pancetta or slab bacon, finely chopped
- 1 medium onion, finely chopped
- 2 celery stalks, finely chopped
- 2 carrots, finely chopped
- 5 garlic cloves, minced
- 2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil, plus more for drizzling
- 2 bay leaves
- 2 sprigs rosemary
- 1 1/2 lb ground pork
- 1 1/2 lb ground beef
- 2 cups milk
- 1 (28 oz) can crushed tomatoes
- 2 cups dry red wine
- Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 1 lb dried tagliatelle
- Fresh ricotta cheese, basil leaves, and grated Parmigiano-Reggiano, for serving

### Method

1. Reconstitute the porcini in boiling water for 20 minutes until tender. Drain and coarsely chop.
2. Purée the mushrooms, pancetta, onion, celery, carrots, and garlic together in a blender.
3. In a heavy-bottomed pot, heat the olive oil with the bay leaves and rosemary until fragrant. Add the purée and cook 5–10 minutes.
4. Increase the heat and add the pork and beef. Cook until no longer pink, breaking up clumps with a wooden spoon.
5. Add the milk and simmer until nearly evaporated, about 10 minutes.
6. Add tomatoes and red wine. Season with salt and pepper. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat, cover, and simmer gently for 1 1/2–2 hours until thick.
7. Cook tagliatelle in salted boiling water until al dente, 8–10 minutes. Drain and toss with the sauce.
8. Serve topped with ricotta, shredded basil, Parmigiano-Reggiano, and a drizzle of olive oil.

## Notes

- The milk softens the acidity of the tomatoes and gives a richer flavor.
- For a richer, thicker sauce, uncover for the last 30 minutes of simmering.
- The sauce improves even more the next day.
- For an authentic pairing, serve with **tagliatelle** and a glass of **Sangiovese**.
