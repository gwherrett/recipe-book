# Chicken Mole Negro (Oaxacan)

**Source:** [Rick Bayless, via Sonoma Magazine (May 2010)](https://www.sonomamag.com/robledos-in-a-state/)

**Total Time:** about 4 hours (sauce can be made days ahead)
**Yield:** 4 servings, with about 4 cups of sauce (leftovers for enchiladas) — a half batch of Bayless' original

Rick Bayless' Oaxacan black mole, served at the 2010 White House state dinner honouring Mexican President Calderón. Four separate purées — nuts, tomato-tomatillo, banana-spice, and chiles — are each fried down until very dark, then simmered with broth and Mexican chocolate. This version is adapted to use only pasillas and guajillos, chipotle adobo purée, almond-sesame dukkah, home-roasted jack-o'-lantern pumpkin seeds, tomatillo sauce and a ripe tomato, red onion, and gluten-free tortillas in place of the corn tortilla and bread. It comes out a deep, dark red rather than true black (see Substitutions below).

---

## Ingredients

### Chiles

- About 2½ oz (7 to 8 medium) dried pasilla chiles (see note on checking these)
- About 2¼ oz (7 to 9 medium) dried guajillo chiles
- About 2 tsp chipotle adobo purée
- Optional, for heat: 1 or 2 dried pequín chiles and/or a pinch of árbol powder
- ½ gluten-free tortilla, torn into small pieces

### Seeds & Nuts

- ¼ cup sesame seeds, plus a few extra for garnish
- ¼ cup roasted, seasoned pumpkin seeds (pepitas) in their shells
- 2 tbsp unskinned or Spanish peanuts
- 2 tbsp almond-sesame dukkah (already toasted — no need to roast)

### Purée Base

- ¼ medium red onion (about 2 oz), cut as one ½-inch-thick slice through the middle
- 2 garlic cloves, unpeeled
- 1 cup tomatillo sauce (salsa verde)
- 1 medium-large (about 6 oz) ripe tomato, cored and roughly chopped
- 1 gluten-free tortilla, toasted until very dark brown and crisp (in place of the bread)
- ⅛ tsp cloves, preferably freshly ground
- ¼ tsp black pepper, preferably freshly ground
- ¼ tsp cinnamon, preferably freshly ground Mexican canela
- A scant ½ tsp oregano, preferably Mexican
- ¼ tsp dried thyme
- ¼ ripe banana (about a 2-inch piece)

### To Finish

- About 1 cup rich-tasting lard or vegetable oil (for frying the chiles)
- 4 cups chicken broth
- ¼ cup (about 1½ oz) finely chopped Mexican chocolate
- 1 or 2 avocado leaves (if you have them)
- Salt, about 1½ tsp depending on the saltiness of the broth (start lower — see Notes)
- Sugar, about 2 tbsp (or a little more)
- 1 large (3½ to 4 lb) chicken, cut into quarters

---

## Instructions

### 1. Getting Started

1. **Seed the chiles:** Pull the stems (and attached seed pods) from the dried pasillas and guajillos, tear them open and shake or scrape out the seeds, collecting them as you go. Leave any pequíns whole. Set the adobo purée aside.

2. **Burn the seeds:** Scoop the seeds into an ungreased 8- to 9-inch skillet with the torn tortilla. Set over medium heat, turn on the exhaust fan, open a window, and toast, shaking the pan regularly, until thoroughly burned to charcoal black, about 15 minutes. (This is very important to the flavour and colour of the mole.) Scrape into a fine-mesh strainer, rinse for about 30 seconds, then transfer to a blender.

3. **Roast the onion and garlic:** Set an ungreased skillet or griddle over medium heat, lay on a piece of foil, and lay the onion slice and garlic on it. Roast until soft and very dark — about 7 to 8 minutes per side for the onion (peel it off the foil to turn it) and about 15 minutes for the garlic, turning frequently. Cool the garlic a bit, peel, and combine with the onion in a large bowl.

4. **Fry and soak the chiles:** Meanwhile, heat the oven to 350°F. Set a small (6- to 7-inch) skillet over medium heat, add a scant 1 cup of the lard or oil (about ½-inch depth), and when hot, fry the dried pasillas and guajillos a couple at a time. Tear the long pasillas into pieces so they lie flat in the small pan. They'll unfurl quickly and release their aroma; after about 30 seconds they'll be well toasted (crisp when cool, but not burnt-smelling). Pasillas are thin and scorch faster than guajillos, so pull them as soon as they smell toasty. Don't fry the pequíns. Drain well, gather into a large bowl, cover with hot tap water and let rehydrate 30 minutes, stirring regularly. Drain, reserving the soaking liquid.

5. **Toast the seeds and tortilla:** While the chiles soak, spread the sesame seeds on one baking sheet and the peanuts on another; put both in the oven. In about 12 minutes the sesame will be dark brown; the peanuts take slightly longer. Lay the whole gluten-free tortilla (the bread replacement) on the peanut sheet and toast until very dark brown and crisp. Check it after 8 minutes, because it can go from brown to burnt quickly. The pumpkin seeds are already roasted; just warm them on the tray for the last few minutes. The dukkah is already toasted and doesn't go in the oven.

6. **Nut purée:** Add the sesame (reserving a few for garnish), pumpkin seeds, peanuts and dukkah to the blender with the burnt seeds and tortilla, plus ¾ cup of the broth. Blend as smooth as you can — give it extra time to break down the pumpkin seed shells. Transfer to a small bowl.

7. **Tomato purée:** Without rinsing the blender, purée the tomatillo sauce and chopped tomato until smooth. The sauce is already liquid, so no broth is needed. Pour into another bowl.

8. **Banana-spice purée:** Again without rinsing, blend the roasted onion and garlic with the toasted gluten-free tortilla (broken up), cloves, black pepper, cinnamon, oregano, thyme, banana and ¼ cup broth until smooth (add a splash of water if the blender struggles). Pour into a small bowl.

9. **Chile purée:** Without rinsing, add all the soaked chiles, the adobo purée, the pequíns (if using) and ½ cup of the soaking liquid; blend smooth and pour into another bowl. (The half batch fits in one go; add a splash more soaking liquid if the blender struggles.)

### 2. From Four Purées to Mole

With the half batch, the purées may reduce a little faster than the times below — go by colour and thickness.

1. **Fry the tomato purée:** In a large (5- to 6-quart) pot, preferably a Dutch oven or cazuela, heat 1½ tbsp of the lard or oil (some of the chile-frying fat is fine) over medium-high. When very hot, add the tomato purée and stir and scrape with a flat wooden spatula for 15 to 20 minutes, until reduced, thick as tomato paste, and darkened to a deep brick-brown.

2. **Add the nut purée:** Stir and scrape until reduced, thick and dark again, about 8 minutes.

3. **Add the banana-spice purée:** Stir and scrape another 7 to 8 minutes, until it simmers back down to a thick mass about the same colour as before.

4. **Add the chile purée:** Stir well and reduce over medium-low until very thick and a very deep red-brown, close to black, about 30 minutes, stirring regularly (but not constantly).

5. **Simmer:** Stir in the remaining 3 cups of broth, the chocolate and the avocado leaves (if using). Partially cover and simmer gently about 1 hour. Season with salt and sugar — this is a sweet mole, and the sugar balances the dark, toasty flavours. Without the sweeter anchos or mulatos, it may need a little more sugar or chocolate, so taste and adjust. For more heat, stir in árbol powder a pinch at a time. Remove the avocado leaves.

6. **Blend and strain:** In batches if needed, in a loosely covered blender, purée until as smooth as possible, then press through a medium-mesh strainer into a large bowl. This is where any remaining bits of pumpkin seed shell get caught. If the mole is thicker than you like, thin it with a little water or leftover chile-soaking liquid.

### 3. Finishing the Dish

1. **Braise the chicken:** Return the mole to the pot and bring to a simmer. Nestle in the leg-and-thigh quarters, partially cover and cook 15 minutes, then add the breast quarters, partially cover and simmer 20 to 25 minutes more, until all the chicken is done.

2. **Serve:** Lift the chicken onto a large warm platter with a slotted spoon. Spoon a generous amount of mole over and around, sprinkle with the reserved sesame seeds, and serve.

---

## Advance Preparation

The mole can be completed through Step 2 several days ahead — it gets better, in fact. Cover and refrigerate. Complete Step 3 shortly before serving.

## Substitutions from the Original

Quantities in both columns are for the half batch.

| Original (Bayless, halved) | This version | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 or 6 dried mulatos (2¾ oz), 3 dried chilhuacles (1 oz) and 3 dried pasillas (1 oz) | About 2½ oz pasillas and 2¼ oz guajillos | Only pasillas and guajillos on hand. The total chile weight is the same (4¾ oz). A roughly even split keeps the bitterness down: the pasillas bring depth, and the guajillos bring fruitiness and a red colour. Expect a slightly less sweet mole, so adjust the sugar and chocolate at the end |
| ½ dried chipotle meco | About 2 tsp chipotle adobo purée | Added straight to the chile purée — not fried or soaked |
| — | Optional pequín chiles or árbol powder | For heat only. They're too small and hot to stand in for any of the base chiles |
| 2 tbsp pecan halves | ¼ cup roasted, seasoned jack-o'-lantern pumpkin seeds (in shell) | Brings in pepitas; extra volume allows for the shells |
| 2 tbsp unskinned almonds | 2 tbsp almond-sesame dukkah | Brings back Bayless' almonds. It's already toasted, salted and spiced (usually with cumin and coriander), so it skips the oven and means using less salt |
| 1 ¼-inch slice white onion | ¼ medium red onion (one ½-inch slice) | Red onion is what's on hand and roasts a little sweeter. The amount is doubled because other mole negro recipes of this size use more onion than Bayless does |
| 8 oz green tomatoes and 2 oz tomatillos | 1 cup tomatillo sauce and 1 ripe tomato | What's on hand. The sauce brings the tartness of the green tomatoes and tomatillos and is already liquid, so that purée needs no broth. The red tomato adds body and a little sweetness, and makes the mole redder |
| ½ corn tortilla (burned with the chile seeds) | ½ gluten-free tortilla | What's on hand; it gets burned with the seeds the same way |
| 1 slice stale bread, toasted very dark | 1 gluten-free tortilla, toasted very dark | Does the same job of thickening the banana-spice purée |

If you can find mulatos another time, Bayless' own chilhuacle-free mix for the half batch is 3 oz (6) mulatos, 1¼ oz (4) pasillas and ½ oz (2) guajillos.

## Notes

- **Check your pasillas:** Some stores label anchos as "pasilla." True pasillas (chile negro) are long, skinny, wrinkled and nearly black. If yours are wide and heart-shaped, they're anchos. They're still fine to use, but the mole will be sweeter, less bitter and earthy, and a little lighter in colour.
- **Colour:** This version comes out a deep, dark red rather than true black. For a darker mole, shift the chiles toward pasillas (about 3 oz pasillas and 1¾ oz guajillos).
- **Salty extras:** The pumpkin seeds, dukkah, adobo purée and tomatillo sauce are all already seasoned, so start with about half the salt at the end and adjust up. The tomatillo sauce may bring some heat too, so taste before adding árbol powder.
- **Pumpkin seed shells:** Blend the nut purée longer than usual, and don't skip the final straining — the medium-mesh strainer removes the shell fibre.
